Long sentences — where the eye reads them

The problem, in one sentence: ผมพูดภาษาไทยได้นิดหน่อย wraps to three lines at the top, and during the count-in you cannot read the top and watch the panel at once. Three answers, each shown idle (left) and mid-take (right), with the rail in place. All three also widen the page on large screens (1080 → 1320 px) so the panels get more time per pixel.

A — the panel takes over during the take

Idle: the sentence is big at the top, fitted to one line (the type scales down for long sentences instead of wrapping). Count-in and recording: the top line drops to a small caption and the You panel shows the syllables large over their slots, with the native dashed line under them; the syllable at the playhead lights up. One reading place at a time, never two.

B — the sentence lives in the panel, always

The top keeps only the IPA, the meaning and the button — no big sentence there at all. The You panel carries the syllables over their slots at all times: before the take as the thing to read (with the native dashed line as the target), during it lit by the playhead, after it above your spectrogram. The sentence appears exactly once, next to the glides it describes. The native panel keeps its small labels.

Smallest change: the sentence stays at the top but scales to a single line (the long one becomes about 2 rem instead of 3.4); the page widens on large screens; the You panel adds the native dashed line and a small count in its corner. Nothing moves during the take, so the eye still travels between the top line and the panel — shorter travel than today, not none.
