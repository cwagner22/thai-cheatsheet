Speaking tab — where the buttons live

Seven controls today, all in one row, all the same weight. They fall into four jobs that are pressed at very different rates: record / try again (every take) · the native voice (Listen, Word by word — a few times per sentence) · my take (Listen to my voice, Download — after a take, sometimes) · the picture (Sensitivity, textbook tones — once, if ever). Each option below places them by that. All show the state after a take.

A — each voice carries its own controls

The native panel gets Listen and Word by word in its corner; your panel gets Listen to my voice and Download in its corner (empty until there is a take). The one big action sits between the sentence and the panels. Picture settings shrink to a footer line. Nothing to hunt for: a control is on the thing it acts on.

B — one command bar, grouped and labelled

Everything stays in one bar under the sentence, but in three labelled groups so the eye reads jobs, not seven equal buttons. Record is first and biggest; "my take" is greyed until there is one; picture settings live behind a single small button that opens a row. Closest to today's layout, least to relearn.

C — one button, the rest are icons on the panels

A single round record button, always in the same place, is the whole interface for the loop you repeat. Play icons sit on each panel (native on top, yours below), so "play" always means "play this". Word by word, Save and the picture settings fold into a small More line — present, out of the way. Best for phones, and for the keyboard: Space records, L plays the native voice, M plays yours.
