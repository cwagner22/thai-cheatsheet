Count-in and recording — what the You panel shows

Left: the count-in. Right: mid-take, playhead moving. In all three the native pitch is drawn dashed on your panel before you speak, so the glide you are about to make is in front of you; the textbook bands stay under the toggle. What differs is where the sentence goes.

A — prompter: each syllable over its own slot

The syllables sit large along the top of the panel, each above the time where the native voice says it — so the sentence reads left to right as time does, and the glide under each syllable is the glide for that syllable. While recording, the playhead reaches a syllable and it lights up; passed ones fade. The count sits small in the corner so it never covers a glide.

B — banner: the sentence across the top, count large in the corner

The whole sentence centred in one line at the top of the panel, IPA under it, the glides beneath; the count is big in the bottom-right where there is rarely a glide. Easiest to read from a distance; the link between a syllable and its glide is by colour only, not by position. While recording the banner stays and the playhead runs under it.
